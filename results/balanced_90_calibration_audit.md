## Threshold assessment

`safe_threshold=0.72` is not optimal as a standalone clinical gate. Raising it would convert some WARNING → SAFE errors back to warnings, but it would not reliably fix BLOCKED → SAFE errors because several blocked cases have confidence 1.0. A score-only threshold cannot separate these classes.

Recommended policy:

1. Retain 0.72 only as a provisional high-confidence boundary.
2. Add a mandatory contraindication/emergency override before SAFE.
3. Treat high-confidence NEUTRAL NLI results as unresolved, not safe entailment.
4. Increase the risk contribution of detected contraindications and require low risk for SAFE.
5. Calibrate warning/block thresholds on a larger balanced validation set; the current `warn_threshold=0.48` is not the active final gate.

## Final conclusion

Retrieval quality contributes to errors: several wrong decisions cite weak or unrelated evidence while still producing high support scores. Self-consistency is not informative because every case has one candidate and therefore score 1.0. NLI executed successfully, but neutral NLI outcomes are not sufficient to establish that a proposed clinical action is safe.

Ready for 350-case benchmark? **NO**. Execution is reliable, but safety performance is not: warning recall is 0%, blocked recall is 36.67%, and 18 dangerous cases were marked SAFE.
